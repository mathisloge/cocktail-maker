= Contracts

A contract states what crosses one boundary between two blocks or parties of the machine, and what each side guarantees to the other.
Each contract has an ID of the form `IF-nn`, and each of its requirements has an ID of the form `IF-nn-Rnn`.
The IDs are stable and never reused.
Other pages and the subprojects refer to a contract or a requirement by its ID.
The boundaries come from the blocks of xref:building-blocks.adoc[chapter 5].

[[IF-01]]
== IF-01: Station to Pod

Sides:: The Station (Station docs) and a Pod (Pod firmware docs).
Both sides are built from one protocol schema.
Kind:: data
Status:: Partly decided

=== What crosses

Frames of the request-response protocol cross a USB CDC serial link.
The Station starts every exchange with a command or a status query.
The Pod answers with a response, an acknowledgement or later an event, always under the `TransactionId` of the request.
The details of the frames and messages stay on the xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page] and in the schema repo.

=== Guarantees and limits

* Transport: USB CDC, and each Pod enumerates as its own USB CDC device (xref:adr:002-pysical-pod-connection.adoc[SYS-002]).
* Cable length is limited to typically a few meters without hubs, and the bandwidth is shared by all Pods (xref:adr:002-pysical-pod-connection.adoc[SYS-002]).
* The Station is the sole initiator, and a Pod never sends an unsolicited command (xref:adr:003-protocol-communication.adoc[SYS-003]).
* Every frame carries a Sync marker, a size, a message ID, a `TransactionId` and a CRC-16 over size through payload.
  There is no byte stuffing (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Framing).
* A `TransactionId` is unique among the pending requests only, and the Pod echoes it on every frame of that exchange (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Transaction Correlation).
* A pour ends with `DispenseFinished`, which reports the measured amount and a `DispenseResult`.
  A pour that ends short is no error, and only `Completed` reaches the requested amount (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Communication Patterns).
* A pour stops after 30 s at the latest, a provisional value to be confirmed on the assembled machine (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], `DispenseResult` table).
* The schema release is 2.1.0, written `0x020100` (schema repo).
  The Station accepts exactly the release it was built against (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Versioning).
* The Station waits for a reply at most as long as the Reply Times table allows.
  The values are 100 ms, 500 ms, 2 s, 35 s, and run time plus 3 s for a calibration run (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Reply Times).
* An unanswered `Ping` or `DeviceInfoRequest` is repeated with a longer timeout (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Reply Times).

=== Fault behaviour

* What the Station does with a frame that has a wrong CRC or an unknown layout is not in any source.
* Every Pod exchange has its own timeout, so the Station notices a silent Pod within a bounded time (xref:station:adr:005-sender-receiver-execution-model.adoc[STA-005]).
  How the request fails is open (IF-01-R06).
* What the Station does with a Pod that stays silent after all repeats, and with the requests that are pending for it, is not in any source.
* A command that a Pod refuses or cannot finish is answered with `Nak` and an `ErrorCode`, and the Station reports it as an error for that Pod (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Request / Acknowledgement).
* A Pod on another protocol release stays unused, the Station names both releases (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page], section Versioning).
* When a dispense fails or the Guest cancels, the Station stops the remaining actions and sends an `EmergencyStop` to every Pod (xref:station::runtime-view.adoc[Station runtime view]).
* What a Pod does when the link breaks during a pour is not in any source.

[[IF-02]]
== IF-02: Power Management Unit to Station

Sides:: The Power Management Unit (System docs) and the Station (Station docs).
Kind:: power
Status:: Partly decided

=== What crosses

A 5 V DC supply and a 24 V DC supply cross from the Power Management Unit to the Station.
The 5 V come from a DC/DC converter that steps down the 24 V DC of the power supply.
The Station computer is the consumer of the 5 V, and the show front is the consumer of the 24 V (xref:adr:016-station-show-front.adoc[SYS-016]).
Whether the touch display draws from the same 5 V is not in any source.

=== Guarantees and limits

* The Station computer is the only 5 V consumer in the power chain (xref:power.adoc[Power page], and xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* The show front draws its power from the same power supply as the Pods (xref:adr:016-station-show-front.adoc[SYS-016] and <<IF-09>>).
* The 5 V are made from the 24 V DC rail by a DC/DC converter, behind the power supply, the main switch and the RCD or circuit breaker (xref:power.adoc[Power page]).
* The power supply is a MeanWell NDR-120-24 with 120 W, 24 V and 5 A, and it also feeds the Pods (xref:hardware.adoc[Hardware page]).
  The Hardware page shows a "5 V Step-Down" and names no part for it.
* A protective earth conductor runs through every stage up to the Station (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* The 5 V for the load cells and servos of a Pod do not come from this supply, because a Pod makes its own 5 V from its 24 V (IF-03).
* Not in any source: the 5 V current limit, the voltage tolerance, the connector, a fuse on the 5 V side.

=== Fault behaviour

* If the single power supply fails, the Station and all Pods lose power at once.
  SYS-004 names this as an accepted weakness (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* A power loss leaves a whole state file on the Station, because it writes each file in one step.
  The Station corrects the fill levels when the Pods come back (xref:station::runtime-view.adoc[Station runtime view]).
* What the Station does on a brown-out of the 5 V is not in any source.

=== Requirements

[cols="2,6,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Requirement | Goals | Met by
| [[IF-02-R01]]IF-02-R01 | The 5 V supply stays inside what the Station computer and its display accept, at every load of the Station. | QG-03 | open
| [[IF-02-R02]]IF-02-R02 | A fault on the 5 V side cannot start a fire or leave a conductor live and reachable. | QG-01 | open
| [[IF-02-R03]]IF-02-R03 | A power cut at any moment leaves the Station able to start again with its data intact. | QG-03 | xref:station::runtime-view.adoc[Station runtime view]
|===

=== Open questions

* Which loads does the 5 V rail carry (Raspberry Pi 5, the touch display, anything else), and which current must it deliver?
  The current stays open until it is measured.
* Which part makes the 5 V, and which fuse or protection sits on it?
* Does the Station stay up for a short time after the mains drops, or does it just stop?
* How are the limits of the Station supply written down, so that a person can check and replace it?

[[IF-03]]
== IF-03: Power Management Unit to Pod

Sides:: The Power Management Unit (System docs) and a Pod (Pod firmware and Mechanics docs).
Kind:: power
Status:: Partly decided

=== What crosses

A 24 V DC supply crosses from the Power Management Unit to each Pod.
This is the only supply a Pod takes.
In the Pod it feeds the pumps, valves and stepper motors, and the Pod controller, and the Pod makes its own 5 V from it.
All Pods share this one 24 V rail.

=== Guarantees and limits

* The rail is 24 V DC from one SMPS, and it directly powers pumps, valves and stepper motors (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* A Pod takes only 24 V DC as input, and it lowers the voltage itself to the 5 V that it needs for the load cells and servos (xref:adr:010-pod-supply-24v.adoc[SYS-010]).
* All Pods share the same 24 V rail, and this needs careful current budgeting and fusing as Pods are added (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* The power supply is a MeanWell NDR-120-24 with 120 W, 24 V and 5 A, for the Station and all Pods together (xref:hardware.adoc[Hardware page]).
* The Hardware page shows the 24 V going to the Pod controller.
* The Pod controller is a BIGTREETECH Octopus V1.1, and its POWER terminal block takes GND and 24 V (positions 3 and 4 from +Y).
  The plug depth is assumed and needs a re-measure on the real part (xref:mechanics::pod-electronics.adoc[Mechanics Pod electronics page]).
* The MOTOR_POWER and POWER terminals are screw terminals, and a screwdriver needs access from +Z.
  The Pod design has to leave that room, and the model has no keep-out for it yet (xref:mechanics::pod-electronics.adoc[Mechanics Pod electronics page]).
* A Pod holds 8 stepper motors 17HE19-2004S, 8 driver modules and 10 load cell units in the bill of materials (xref:mechanics::structure.adoc[Mechanics product structure page]).
* A protective earth conductor runs through every stage to every Pod (xref:power.adoc[Power page]).
* An Operator connects or disconnects the supply of a Pod only while the machine is off, and the main switch comes first.
  The USB cable of a Pod may be replugged while the machine runs (xref:adr:012-pods-changed-switched-off.adoc[SYS-012]).
* Not in any source: the current of a Pod, the number of Pods the supply can carry, the fuse values, the power connector of a Pod.

=== Fault behaviour

* If the single SMPS fails, all Pods and the Station lose power at once (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* What a Pod does when the 24 V drops or returns during a pour is not in any source.
* Which part detects an overload of the shared rail is not in any source.

=== Requirements

| ID | Requirement | Goals | Met by

| [[IF-03-R02]]IF-03-R02 | A short circuit or overload in one Pod is cut off before it harms a person, damages another Pod or stops the other Pods. | QG-01, QG-03 | open

=== Open questions

* How many Pods must the power supply carry, and which current does one Pod draw at most?
* Which fuse or protection sits in front of each Pod?
* Which power connector does a Pod use?
* How is the power need of a Pod written down, so that a developer can check the rail budget before a Pod is added?
* The power budget stays open and is a risk for chapter 11 later (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004] names it).

[[IF-04]]
== IF-04: Power Grid to Power Management Unit

Sides:: The Power Grid (external) and the Power Management Unit (System docs).
Kind:: power
Status:: Partly decided

=== What crosses

Mains AC power of 230 V and 50 Hz crosses from the Power Grid into the machine through the mains inlet.
The Power Management Unit takes it through the main switch and the protective device to the power supply.

=== Guarantees and limits

* The machine has one single external 230 V AC mains connection, and it takes no other mains voltage (xref:power.adoc[Power page], xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004] and xref:adr:011-german-mains.adoc[SYS-011]).
* The mains is 230 V AC at 50 Hz, the standard voltage in Germany (xref:adr:011-german-mains.adoc[SYS-011]).
* The chain is an IEC C14 inlet, a main switch, a residual current and overcurrent protection device, and then the SMPS (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* The main switch is one clearly labeled point where an Operator fully disconnects the machine from mains (xref:power.adoc[Power page]).
* The protective device acts before any DC conversion (xref:power.adoc[Power page]).
* A continuous protective earth conductor runs from the inlet through every stage (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* Not in any source: the power rating of the machine, the mains plug and cord, the standards that apply, the inrush current, the behaviour on a mains brown-out.

=== Fault behaviour

* A residual current fault or an overcurrent is cut off by the protective device before the power supply (xref:power.adoc[Power page]).
* The Operator can cut all power with the main switch (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004]).
* The Hardware page shows no protective device between the main switch and the power supply, and the Power page does.

[[IF-05]]
== IF-05: Bottle to Pod

Sides:: The Raw ingredients in their bottles (supplied by the Operator) and a Pod (Pod firmware and Mechanics docs).
Kind:: fluid
Status:: Partly decided

=== What crosses

An ingredient flows from a bottle through a supply line into the pump or valve of one dispenser.
The weight of the bottle also crosses into the load cell of that dispenser, which weighs it.
The pump side of the line is designed, and the bottle side (bottle holder, supply line, how a bottle is changed) is not.

=== Guarantees and limits

* An Operator refills a bottle from the top (xref:adr:007-calibration-and-fill-level.adoc[SYS-007]) or lifts it out to refill, empty or rinse it (xref:adr:015-food-contact-plastic-bottles.adoc[SYS-015]).
* One load cell unit (M5Stack Unit Mini Scales) weighs one bottle, and a Pod holds 10 bottles (xref:mechanics::index.adoc[Mechanics index page] and xref:mechanics::structure.adoc[Mechanics product structure page]).
* A Pod has 8 pumps and 2 valves, and the valves are for carbonated drinks (xref:adr:013-pod-pumps-and-valves.adoc[SYS-013]).
* Only food-grade tubes, stainless steel parts, glass and declared HDPE or PP bottles, caps and fittings touch a liquid, and no printed part does (xref:adr:015-food-contact-plastic-bottles.adoc[SYS-015]).
* The pump piece is a silicone tube 7/5 mm of Shore A 60 and 215 mm long, with a food-safe 5 mm barb at each end (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page] and xref:mechanics:adr:002-pump-fixed-occlusion-bed.adoc[CM3D-002]).
* The tube pieces and fittings are bought as a silicone tube 7/5 mm, Shore A 60, and a 5 mm barbed fitting that is food-grade (xref:mechanics::purchasing.adoc[Mechanics purchasing page]).
* A tube change on the installed pump leaves the supply and outlet lines untouched (xref:mechanics:adr:002-pump-fixed-occlusion-bed.adoc[CM3D-002]).
* A pump delivers 3.64 mL per revolution (80 % efficiency assumed) and 9.1 mL/s at 150 rpm, and its maximum is 12.1 mL/s at 200 rpm.
  Calibration with the load cell evens out the real flow (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* The load cell reads to about 1 g and drifts by up to about 20 g in an hour as measured (xref:adr:007-calibration-and-fill-level.adoc[SYS-007]).
* The beam of the load cell is to be checked under a full load of 5 kg, and the 1 mm gap around its top half is an assumption (xref:mechanics::risks-and-technical-debt.adoc[Mechanics risks page]).
* The bottles are HDPE or PP with a declaration, and the Mechanics round plans square 2 l wide-neck bottles (xref:adr:015-food-contact-plastic-bottles.adoc[SYS-015]).
* Not in any source: the bottle product, the bottle holder, the supply line length and diameter, the connection of a bottle to its line.

=== Fault behaviour

* A pour that moves next to nothing ends with a `BottleEmpty` result, because the bottle is empty or the line is blocked (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page]).
  The Station asks the Operator to refill (xref:station::runtime-view.adoc[Station runtime view]).
* A bottle refilled while the Station is off stays unnoticed until someone corrects its fill level (xref:adr:007-calibration-and-fill-level.adoc[SYS-007]).
* What happens to the liquid when a line or barb comes loose is not in any source.
  The pump housing has a drain in its bottom wall, through which a leak leaves (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).

[[IF-06]]
== IF-06: Pod to glass through the Station fluid hub

Sides:: A Pod (Pod firmware and Mechanics docs) and the Station (Station docs).
Kind:: fluid
Status:: Not designed yet

=== What crosses

The ingredient flows from the outlet of a dispenser through fluid tubing to the Station fluid hub.
The hub collects the flow of all Pods and leads it into the glass in the glass placement area.
This is the whole food-contact path from the Pod outlet to the glass.

=== Guarantees and limits

* The Hardware page shows an edge "delivers cocktail through fluid tubing" from the dispenser unit to the Station fluid hub, and it names the hub "manifold / collection" (xref:hardware.adoc[Hardware page]).
* The Station houses the glass placement area (xref:naming-conventions.adoc[Naming conventions page]).
* All food-contact parts and all flow paths use food-safe materials (xref:quality-goals.adoc[Quality goals page] and xref:safety.adoc[Safety page]).
* Only food-grade tubes, stainless steel parts, glass and declared HDPE or PP bottles, caps and fittings touch a liquid, and no printed part does (xref:adr:015-food-contact-plastic-bottles.adoc[SYS-015]).
* The pump has two 5 mm barb spigots below its housing, 8.8 mm long outside the wall, and the tube leaves toward -Y (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* A leak in the pump housing leaves through a drain in its bottom wall (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* A pour stops after 30 s at the latest, a provisional value to be confirmed on the assembled machine (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page]).
* The Hardware page shows the load cell as weighing "bottle / glass".
  The System docs say only that it weighs the bottle (xref:adr:007-calibration-and-fill-level.adoc[SYS-007]).
* Not in any source: the hub design, the tube lengths and diameters after the pump, the outlet position, a glass sensor, the material of the hub, how the path is cleaned.

=== Fault behaviour

* Not designed yet.
* Cleaning with hot water needs a pump that runs until someone stops it, and emptying the tubes needs a pump that runs backwards.
  The protocol and the Pod firmware support neither today (xref:station::risks-and-technical-debt.adoc[Station risks page]).

[[IF-07]]
== IF-07: Pod to Station, mechanical

Sides:: A Pod (Mechanics docs and Pod firmware docs) and the Station (Mechanics docs and Station docs).
Kind:: mechanical
Status:: Not designed yet

=== What crosses

A Pod stands hidden from the Guests, free from the Station, and cables and tubes join the two (xref:adr:016-station-show-front.adoc[SYS-016]).
The cables are the USB cable (IF-01), the 24 V supply (IF-03) and the protective earth conductor, and the fluid tubing leads to the hub (IF-06).
Chapter 5 draws this arrow as "is cabled to, stands hidden".
Neither housing exists yet.

=== Guarantees and limits

* A Pod is a self-contained and physically independent module, so it can be inspected, recalibrated or swapped out as a single unit (xref:adr:001-system-split.adoc[SYS-001]).
* A machine has one Station and one or more Pods (xref:naming-conventions.adoc[Naming conventions page]).
* Pods are connectable and removable by an Operator without special tools (xref:adr:002-pysical-pod-connection.adoc[SYS-002]).
* The USB cable is limited to typically a few meters without hubs (xref:adr:002-pysical-pod-connection.adoc[SYS-002]).
* The Mechanics docs plan the Pod housing and the Station for later (xref:mechanics::index.adoc[Mechanics index page] and xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* The enclosures will be printed in several PETG colours (xref:mechanics:adr:014-materials-and-filaments.adoc[CM3D-014]).
  Only the Station follows the UI palette (xref:adr:017-station-colours.adoc[SYS-017]).
* Not in any source: how a Pod is mounted, the weight of a Pod with its bottles, the cable set and its connectors, the cable routing, the position of the Station fluid hub.

=== Fault behaviour

* Not designed yet.
* The pump housing leads a leak out through a drain in its bottom wall (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
  Where that liquid goes next is not in any source.

[[IF-08]]
== IF-08: Operator to Pod

Sides:: The Operator (a person, who follows the service steps) and a Pod (Mechanics docs).
Kind:: mechanical
Status:: Partly decided

=== What crosses

The Operator's hands and tools reach into the Pod to refill bottles, to change a pump tube, to change an occlusion gap and to connect or remove the Pod.
Safety information runs the other way, as guards and warnings.
The pump side is designed, and the Pod housing is not.

=== Guarantees and limits

* The pump is a closed box, and its lid is the guard (xref:mechanics:adr:004-pump-closed-housing-bearing-lid.adoc[CM3D-004]).
* A tube change needs a 2.5 mm hex driver with a straight shaft of at least 100 mm, and it starts with stopping the pump and draining the line (xref:mechanics::pump-service.adoc[Mechanics pump service page]).
  The Operator removes three lid screws, and the bed screws are reached through the top wall.
* The service access of a pump needs 35 mm free in front of the lid face over the lid outline, and 130 mm free above the top face for the hex driver (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* One 2.5 mm hex key fits every M3 screw of the pump (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* The pump must never run without its lid, because the nip between roller and tube can trap a finger (xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* The pump has no interlock, so every tube or gap change starts with opening the lid (xref:mechanics:adr:002-pump-fixed-occlusion-bed.adoc[CM3D-002]).
* The lid is the precaution against the risk of the moving pump parts (xref:mechanics:adr:004-pump-closed-housing-bearing-lid.adoc[CM3D-004]).
* The Pod gets only 24 V DC, and mains power stops in the Station (xref:adr:004-power-architecture.adoc[SYS-004] and xref:hardware.adoc[Hardware page]).
* The screw terminals of the Pod controller need screwdriver access from +Z, and the model has no keep-out for it yet (xref:mechanics::pod-electronics.adoc[Mechanics Pod electronics page]).
* Not in any source: the housing, the access door of the Pod, a guard for the other moving parts, a bottle change procedure.

=== Fault behaviour

* Residual risk: no interlock exists (xref:mechanics:adr:002-pump-fixed-occlusion-bed.adoc[CM3D-002]), so a finger can be trapped when the lid is opened while the pump can still move.
  The lid, the rule "never run the pump without the lid" and the service steps are the precaution that mitigates it (xref:mechanics:adr:004-pump-closed-housing-bearing-lid.adoc[CM3D-004] and xref:mechanics::pump.adoc[Mechanics pump page]).
* The Station can stop every pump and valve of a Pod with an `EmergencyStop`, and a running tare or load cell calibration carries on (xref:protocol.adoc[Protocol page]).
* Not designed yet: what stops a Pod when an Operator opens it.

[[IF-09]]
== IF-09: Station software to show front

Sides:: The Station software (Station docs) and the show front hardware (Mechanics docs).
Kind:: data
Status:: Partly decided

=== What crosses

Direct commands cross from the Station software to the show front, one per effect channel: air on or off, and light with a colour and a brightness.
The show front carries them out and computes nothing of its own.
Its power comes from the Power Management Unit (<<IF-02>>).

=== Guarantees and limits

* The Station software computes every effect from its own events (xref:adr:016-station-show-front.adoc[SYS-016]).
* The show front has hardware and bought parts and no effect logic (xref:adr:016-station-show-front.adoc[SYS-016]).
* The mechanical design states the number of effect channels, the link to the Station and the load of the show front.
* No Pod takes part, and the protocol of <<IF-01>> does not change (xref:adr:016-station-show-front.adoc[SYS-016]).
* Not in any source: the link, the number of channels, the load.

=== Fault behaviour

* When the Station stops or the link breaks, the show front keeps its last state until someone switches the machine off (xref:adr:016-station-show-front.adoc[SYS-016]).
  Air and light are harmless in that state.
* A fault of the show front never stops or delays a drink (IF-09-R02).
